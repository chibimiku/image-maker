Render the requested subject with the delicate ink-and-transparent-colour language of CLAMP's original colour illustrations, focusing on Cardcaptor Sakura and Chobits. This is an initial style specification to be reconciled against selected original illustrations, not a verified extraction. Transfer drawing and colouring behaviour while keeping the requested identity, age, intrinsic colours, clothing, objects, pose, setting and image proportions.

LINE LANGUAGE
Use fine, controlled, elastic ink contours with graceful changes of pressure and long, continuous curves. Give the outer silhouette and important overlaps slightly more weight than interior detail. Taper fine strokes into clear endpoints. Keep small structural lines sparse and purposeful; avoid rough sketch residue, uniform vector outlines and dense disconnected scratches. Dark contours may remain crisp against pale colour without outlining every painted transition.

FACE AND EYES
Use economical two-dimensional facial construction: a gently narrowing jaw, lightly indicated nose and small, precisely placed mouth. Preserve the subject's apparent age and expression. Treat large expressive eyes through clear upper-lid arcs, selective tapered lash accents, lighter lower lids, and an iris that reads as a coherent graphic shape. Organize dark upper values, a readable pupil and a few decisive light shapes rather than jewel facets or dense glitter. Eye size, face length and lid roundness are adjustable between the more buoyant Sakura direction and the quieter, more elongated Chobits direction. Do not turn these differences into a mandatory facial template or change the requested eye colour.

HAIR
Construct hair from coherent flowing masses, then divide them into tapering locks with a small number of long interior strokes. Maintain the requested hairstyle, length, volume and direction. Use rhythmic curves and clear overlaps to create movement; concentrate fine strands near separations and tips instead of filling every lock with parallel lines. Shape highlights as integrated pale bands or reserved light areas following the lock curvature, with gentle colour transitions. Avoid metallic strips, plastic gloss and tangled luminous threads.

COLOUR AND LIGHT
Suggest thin transparent colour layers over a light ground, with controlled soft transitions and selective clean colour boundaries. Keep local colours recognizable. Let broad light areas breathe, while dark ink and a few richer accents preserve clarity. Use light, tinted shadows and restrained tonal modelling rather than deep sculptural rendering. Softness must come from colour application, not global blur. A pale or warm ground is optional, not an instruction to bleach the whole image, recolour every subject pink, or remove a dark palette required by the scene. Do not invent a theatrical light source or heavy rim glow.

FABRIC AND MATERIALS
Describe fabric through the relationship between contour, hanging weight, overlapping layers and long folds. When lace, gathers or ribbon are requested, draw readable construction and repeated shapes with a deliberate rhythm; do not add them to unrelated clothing. Distinguish matte cloth, translucent material and small reflective accessories through restrained differences in edge and highlight behaviour. Retain opacity where the source material is opaque. Avoid photorealistic pores, noisy microtexture and glossy game-render surfaces.

SPACE AND COMPOSITION
Keep a clear hierarchy of concentrated detail and quieter open areas. Let silhouettes and overlapping curves carry the composition. Decorative flat shapes and airy negative space may support an illustration when compatible with the requested scene, but do not automatically replace an existing environment with an empty poster. Preserve the source layout during repainting. More elongated proportions are optional for newly designed subjects and must never override a specified age, body type, pose or source anatomy.

CONTENT BOUNDARY
Do not automatically add cherry blossoms, wings, feathers, magic circles, cards, staffs, mechanical ear pieces, elaborate dresses or copied costume patterns. These are optional content choices, not universal rendering rules. Do not copy Sakura, Chi, another reference character, their colours or their compositions. Avoid text, signatures and watermarks. Exclude heavy impasto, photorealistic anatomy, three-dimensional plastic shading, indiscriminate sparkle and the assumption that every image must have the same pastel palette.

DATASET INTERPRETATION FOR PUBLISHER COVERS
These references are published covers. Exclude cover lettering, enclosing frames, volume numbers, uniform publisher margins and ornamental book-design layouts from the extracted painting invariants, even where they recur in most samples. The blue Chobits border and salmon/gold Sakura border are edition design, not required illustration rendering. Observe the actual drawn and painted areas inside them. Treat differences between the two series as variable traits when a common rendering rule is unsupported; do not force every work into one eye aspect ratio, body proportion or palette. Observe large coherent forms as well as fine line and wash detail; increasing decorative density is not automatic improvement.
